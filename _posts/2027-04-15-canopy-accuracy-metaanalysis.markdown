Accuracy figures for satellite- and airborne-derived forest canopy height and aboveground biomass are reported across widely incompatible conventions. Studies differ in the error metric used (RMSE, relative RMSE, R², bias, and combinations thereof), in the reference-data regime against which error is computed (field plots, airborne lidar, terrestrial laser scanning, or another satellite product), and in how — or whether — results are stratified by forest type. The consequence is that a practitioner choosing a sensor stack cannot compare a reported 4.2 m RMSE in a boreal conifer stand against a reported R² of 0.71 in tropical dense forest, because neither the metric nor the reference basis nor the biome is held constant. Published accuracy is abundant; comparable published accuracy is not.

Existing reviews do not resolve this. They are organised by sensing modality and report each study's numbers as the original authors framed them, which reproduces the incompatibility rather than removing it.

This meta-analysis introduces a harmonisation protocol and applies it to the 2020–2026 literature. The protocol has four components: inclusion and exclusion criteria that require quantitative metrics and a mappable reference-data regime; metric normalisation to a common basis (RMSE in metres for canopy height, tonnes per hectare for biomass, plus relative RMSE, R² and bias), excluding rather than imputing where a study reports too little to normalise; stratification by forest type across tropical dense, temperate deciduous, boreal conifer and plantation; and within-stratum comparison only, which controls the apples-versus-oranges risk that cross-biome aggregation introduces. Modality classes assessed are optical multispectral, spaceborne and airborne lidar, SAR, and fused multi-modal pipelines.

An initial sweep identified approximately 165 candidate studies, expanded to 182 unique identifiers for ingestion, of which 154 are currently indexed in the working reference collection. Target screening volume is 200–250 studies with an anticipated 40–60% exclusion rate, yielding an expected 80–150 usable studies. Table extraction is semi-automated, with manual verification against the source PDF before any value enters the harmonised database.

The intended contributions are threefold: a reusable harmonisation protocol other groups can apply to future retrievals; stratified accuracy tables giving normalised error by modality within each forest type; and a prescriptive finding mapping forest type to the modality combination that minimises normalised error in that stratum.
